FAA moves to end the 53-year overland supersonic ban, replacing it with a noise standard Federal Register A proposed rule would allow supersonic flight over US land as long as no sonic boom reaches the ground.

The FAA published a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking on July 2 to repeal the 1973 blanket ban on civil supersonic flight over US land, swapping a speed prohibition for a noise-based standard: overland flight above Mach 1 is fine as long as surface overpressure stays under 0.11 psf — effectively, no boom reaches the ground. It's a meaningful regulatory unlock for Boom Supersonic and others betting on "boomless" cruise techniques, and a rare case of rules catching up to technology rather than lagging it. Comments are due August 17, with a follow-on takeoff/landing noise rule expected in December. The bigger signal is a shift toward performance-based regulation — govern the outcome (noise at the surface), not the method (speed) — exactly how you'd want to regulate a fast-moving frontier technology.
