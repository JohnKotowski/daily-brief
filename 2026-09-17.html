Solo dev post-trains a 4B Qwen to write Postgres query plans 45% faster on average, 81% on the best case HN · 537Reward signal = Postgres's own execution time. Started from a model that couldn't produce a valid plan for 99 of 113 queries.

Rohan Bansal's write-up is the cleanest small-model RL story in a while: SFT on ~500 GPT-6 Astra agent trajectories, then a GRPO-style loop where each rollout emits a pg_hint_plan hint, Postgres runs it against its default plan, and the latency delta is the reward. Across 113 join-heavy IMDb queries the 4B model lands a 44.7% mean latency reduction, up to 81% on individual queries, on a 2×H100 node with desk-side Postgres containers and a noise-controlled measurement rig. The generalisable lesson: when the environment can score you exactly, a tiny model plus RL beats a frontier model plus prompting.

Intel breaks the 1.58-bit "barrier" for ternary LLMs — because up to half the weights are zero, you can pack them in as little as 1.485 bits arXiv · HN 201BITCOS: a presence bitmap plus a compacted sign vector, with AVX-512 / AVX2 / Xe2 unpacking kernels.

Ternary weights are assumed to cost log₂(3) ≈ 1.585 bits each (1.625 with five-trit packing). Georganas, Heinecke and Dubey measured 29 ternary models and found zeros make up as much as 51.5% of the weights, so their BITCOS layout — one bit for "is it non-zero," then only the signs of the non-zeros — costs 2−z bits per weight for zero-density z. It beats five-trit packing on 26 of 29 models and ships optimised unpacking for CPU and Intel GPUs. Small paper, but it's the sort of thing that decides whether a 70B ternary model fits on a laptop.
