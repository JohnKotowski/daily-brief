The oral tradition that built software may not survive AI Slashdot The undocumented "why" behind design decisions lives in people, not repos — and AI-written code may quietly erode it.

The argument: software engineering runs on a vast layer of undocumented, person-to-person institutional knowledge — the reasons behind design choices that never make it into the code — and AI coding tools can't capture it and may actively dissolve it as more code is generated than narrated. It's a sharp meta-take on what's lost when the humans who carried the "why" stop being in the loop. SpaceX's vow to loft 1 million AI satellites could spark a "doomsday dive" Forbes Musk wants to start launching a million orbital data-center satellites in 2028 — critics warn of cascading collision risk.

Musk has committed to begin launching one million AI data-center satellites via Starship starting 2028 — the literal "compute in orbit" bet. Critics warn the scale could sharply worsen orbital congestion and Kessler-cascade collision risk, where one crash spawns debris that triggers others. It's the supply side of the same equation as the Ohio backlash above: if the cheapest land on Earth won't permit the buildout, the play is to move it above the atmosphere — with externalities (debris, congestion, astronomy) nobody has fully priced. Filed under "watch this become a regulatory fight," not "happening tomorrow."

University of Rochester researchers (Chunlei Guo's group) built a solar desalination system using laser-textured metal panels that evaporate seawater while automatically shedding salt deposits rather than clogging.
