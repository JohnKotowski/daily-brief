Mystery solved: the once-a-minute frequency sag seen on power grids from Europe to Australia is data-centre and grid-battery UPS systems all checking their state of charge on the same synchronised clock TechCrunchIn Ireland the per-minute dip quadrupled from 3 mHz in 2016 to 12 mHz in 2021 and now exceeds 20 mHz, against a 100–200 mHz tolerance.

Dirk Witthaut's team at Forschungszentrum Jülich, asked by transmission operator Tennet Germany, first ruled out hydropower resonance, then installed more than 80 power-quality meters across their own campus and found the per-minute spike on the transformer feeding a battery storage system. Internet-synced UPSes and grid-scale batteries poll state of charge exactly on the minute, and enough of them now exist to be visible as a grid-wide heartbeat. The fix is randomising the polling schedule; the complication is that IoT devices show the same pattern and fewer than 3% run current firmware. A good story on its own, and a reminder that "data centres and the grid" is not only a capacity question.

Growth Unhinged: Clay's head of GTM engineering walks through the 17 automated workflows Clay runs on its own 250,000 Salesforce accounts and 850,000 users, from a 0–100 fit score to a weekly "top 5 accounts" model per rep Growth Unhinged · Kyle Poyar · SunClay's top-ARR customers have 10%+ of headcount in sales or GTM, so that became a weighted input to the score.

Poyar interviews Osman Sheikhnureldin, an early Clay employee, on how Clay uses Clay without losing the relationships half of the GTM barbell. Every account is enriched with firmographics plus custom AI research fields (personas sold to, PLG vs sales-led, estimated TAM) and rolled into a 0–100 fit score written to Salesforce. Routing then splits by fit tier and contact seniority: a decision-maker at a qualified account books an AE straight from the website, a non-decision-maker goes to a "ClayDR", an unqualified visitor goes self-serve. A weekly propensity score (fit, predicted contract value taken as the 90th-percentile ARR of the 25 most similar closed-won deals, intent, engagement) flags each rep's top five accounts. The predicted-contract-value trick is directly reusable for Pulse: nearest-neighbour closed-won deals as a value prior, which is also what a pricing-pages corpus is good for.

Latent Space: Stanford's CS146S rewrote its homework because students could one-shot last year's assignments with agents, so Mihail Eric now has them land patches with real open-source maintainers Latent Space · Sun · one-million-subscriber special"You buy to learn, and you build to keep": Monaco kept buying Devin for code review but built its own agent workspaces.

Eric teaches The Modern Software Developer at Stanford and leads AI at Monaco. When he surveyed sophomores and juniors, every one was already coding with an agent, so the course's nine assignments plus capstone were rebuilt around contributing to a live open-source repo where the maintainer reviews the student's patch: the agent can write the feature, but the student still has to learn whether the maintainer wants it and what it breaks. On the Monaco side the team built Hal, an agent engineers ask to investigate outages and open PRs and salespeople ask how the product works. Eric's prediction is that open-source repos become agent-run software factories where the scarce job is deciding which change belongs, which is the same claim as Saturday's Hightouch card from the other direction: the human is the router, not the worker.
