Carter Leffer pointed GPT-6 Astra at Crypto Cellar Research's list of unbroken WWII Enigma intercepts and let it choose. It selected MVUEH as the most promising, wrote Python and C++ Enigma simulation and bombe-attack software from scratch, and used the repeated place-name "ROSENOW ROSENOW" as a crib to run a successful attack. The plaintext turned out to be near-identical to another message from the same day (Nr. 173), differing by twelve letters through an enciphering error and a repeated signature — which is exactly why it had been so hard: the transcription was corrupted and the wheel turnover was in an unusual position. The HN thread's best line: the interesting part isn't that a model can run a bombe, it's that it decided which message to attack and why.

What California is learning from solar panels built over irrigation canals: less evaporation, cooler panels, no new land — and the real constraints are maintenance and money KQED · HN 210 ptsProject Nexus in the Central Valley is the first US test of a decade-old idea; the early data is in.

KQED's report on Project Nexus, the Turlock Irrigation District pilot that canopies canals with solar panels. The theory has always been elegant — shade the water and you cut evaporation, the water cools the panels and they generate more, and you use right-of-way the state already owns instead of farmland. The early results support the physics; the harder lessons are operational: canal-side mounting is more expensive per watt than flat ground, cleaning and access are awkward, and the economics depend on the value you put on saved water in a drought state. The HN discussion split between "this should be everywhere" and engineers pointing at the cost-per-watt line. A good template for the kind of infrastructure that only pencils out once you price the second benefit.
