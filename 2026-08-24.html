Quantinuum's Helios: 98 trapped-ion qubits, and a computation supercomputers can't do Slashdot / Nature The largest trapped-ion machine built. A four-way X-junction lets tasks run in parallel — but the workloads are still benchmarks.

Quantinuum's Helios runs 98 qubits as ions suspended in electromagnetic fields, making it the largest trapped-ion quantum computer built to date. Two advances carry it: a four-way X-junction architecture that allows genuinely parallel task execution, and a new runtime layer that optimises how data is routed through the trap. The company says Helios performed computations that "cannot be performed even on the largest supercomputers using known methods" — while acknowledging, to its credit, that these are still benchmark problems with no practical application. Published in Nature, which is the reason to take it more seriously than the usual quantum press cycle.

The FDA has cleared a blood test to aid Alzheimer's evaluation, work coming out of WashU Medicine. Kaspersky's Securelist documents malware embedded directly in the firmware of Android-based automotive head units — the aftermarket infotainment screens sold by the million. Because it ships pre-installed, none of the usual user-behaviour defences apply: there's no bad click to avoid, and the owner has no realistic way to inspect or re-flash the unit. Head units sit on the vehicle bus and hold location history, paired phones, and contacts, which makes this a meaningfully worse place for persistent malware than a handset. A clean illustration of how far upstream supply-chain compromise has moved.
