Raschka: GPT-6 Astra probably loops its transformer blocks — and its short reasoning traces are a feature, not a cover-upSebastian Raschka · HN 418 Circumstantial but strong evidence that Astra reuses the same 22 blocks several times per token, buying depth without parameters. The "hidden reasoning" worry, he argues, is a misread.

A looped transformer feeds hidden states back through the same stack of blocks rather than adding new ones — more compute per token at a fixed parameter count. Raschka's evidence that Astra does this is a report in The Information plus OpenAI's chief scientist saying the model's "depth of the computation graph" is within a factor of two of GPT-4, which only adds up if blocks are reused. He rates it "highly likely" but unconfirmed. On the alarm that Astra's shorter chain-of-thought means it is hiding its reasoning, his answer is the stronger-student analogy: better models need less scratch work, and the chief scientist has said the looping has nothing to do with the chain-of-thought changes. His bottom line: looping probably helps Astra modestly; the training recipe matters more.

Every: "To Read—Or Not to Read the Code?" — the irony of automation, and four habits to keep your judgment from rottingEvery · Kieran Klaassen The better the agent gets, the less you look, and the less you can tell when it's wrong. Klaassen's answer is to read code to learn, not to police.

The "irony of automation" is the 1980s finding that the more capable a system, the worse its human operator gets at catching its failures — vigilance and skill both atrophy. Klaassen (who runs 44 folder-agents at Cora) argues agentic coding is that curve on fast-forward, and that the fix is not reviewing every diff but deliberately reading to understand: keep a gap list of things you didn't grasp in merged code, ask the agent for the system mechanics rather than the change, dig up the decision history behind a design, and quiz yourself on what changed. His close is that technical understanding is what sharpens product judgment, so it's worth paying for in time even when the agent doesn't need you.
