The core problem is attribution: a bank's fraud systems see a session, not an intent, so an agent paying a bill on your instruction looks the same as one acting on a prompt injection. Canada's Bill C-22, the Lawful Access Act, has passed the House and is before the Senate: compelled technical assistance including built-in law-enforcement access points, metadata retention of up to a year, and expanded data sharing with foreign police, including US agencies, that bypasses Canadian judicial review. EFF · HN 410 pts Local and broad at once. For a company holding customer data in Canada, the retention and foreign-sharing clauses are the ones to read.

EFF's piece is a call to action aimed at the Senate stage, which is the last stop. The technical-assistance clause is the controversial one: it can require providers to build and maintain access capabilities, the same concept the UK's Online Safety Act fight turned on. If it passes as written, every Canadian SaaS with a hosted database inherits a new compliance surface.

Gerald Gabrielse's Northwestern team trapped a single positron for a month, potentially indefinitely, using a sodium-22 source, tungsten moderation and a Penning trap near absolute zero — setting up a direct comparison of positron and electron magnetic moments as a test of matter–antimatter asymmetry. Scientific American · HN Precision physics with a clear next experiment, which is rarer than it sounds.

Antimatter normally annihilates on contact with anything, so holding one particle for weeks is mostly an engineering story about vacuum and cooling. The payoff is the measurement that follows: any difference between the positron's and the electron's magnetic moment beyond the Standard Model's prediction would be a crack in the theory of why the universe is made of matter at all.
