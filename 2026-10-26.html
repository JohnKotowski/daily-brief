Microsoft "Decision-1": a 9B decision model post-trained from Qwen 3.5, for routing and verification, not chat Hacker News → shattered.ioScores a fixed list of options in one forward pass and returns a probability for each. Hosted API only.

Decision-1 is not a generator: you hand it a list of answer options and it returns a probability for each in a single pass. Microsoft's named uses are routing, classification, prioritisation, verification and workflow control (support-ticket routing, moderation, pass/fail checks on agent output). It is post-trained from Alibaba's open-weight Qwen3.5-9B, available through Foundry and OpenRouter, and the weights themselves are not distributed; the 36-benchmark and speed figures are vendor-reported. Two things to take from it: Microsoft reaching for a Chinese open base for its own small-model line, and a purpose-built "judge" model for agent pipelines becoming a product in its own right, which is a cheaper verifier than calling a frontier model to grade itself.

IBM's CEO: open-weight models are now "irresistibly good and cheap" to run on-prem Hacker News → The RegisterArvind Krishna on the Q3 call: 10–25% of the token cost, about 90% as effective, 227 points on HN.

On IBM's earnings call Krishna said closed frontier models were "plainly superior" in 2024, that the gap started shrinking in late 2025, and that open-weight models now run at 10–25% of the frontier token cost while being roughly 90% as effective, which is enough for most enterprise tasks (his examples: summarising email, examining a contract) in two or three racks on-prem. The HN thread, full of people already running Qwen- and Llama-class models in production, mostly agrees with the premise. A decade-old CNN beats GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 at skin-cancer diagnosis, 62% to 42% Hacker News → Lynch Lab546 points: on a narrow, well-labelled task the small specialist still wins.

Dermatologist Magnus Lynch drew 200 biopsy-proven lesions from the public MILK10k dataset and asked GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1 and a fine-tuned Inception v3 CNN (Google, 2015; fine-tuned on 68,011 dermatology images on one H100) for one of six diagnoses. The CNN got 125 right, the two frontier models 84 and 85. His own caveats: small numbers, each LLM case run once, and the CNN was deliberately not state of the art. The lesson HN keeps relearning is the general one: with good labels, a purpose-built classifier is cheaper, faster, more auditable and more accurate; the LLM's advantage is breadth and explanation, and products that need both increasingly pair the two.

Innovation & Infra

Drew Breunig: "The State of Context Engineering" Hacker News → dbreunig.comHis AGNTCon talk: the same six tactics still cover the field; memory and forgetting are the open problem. 173 points.

Breunig, who coined much of the vocabulary around context rot and poisoning, takes stock a year on. His six tactics (context augmentation, tool loadout, quarantine via subagents, pruning, offloading, and summarisation, "which everyone knows as compaction") have, he says, surprisingly stayed the same even as the challenges changed, with pruning increasingly moving into harnesses like Claude Code and Codex. What nobody has solved is memory: "strategic forgetting" is still an open question. It is the best single-page orientation for anyone designing an agent that runs long sessions over messy enterprise data, which is what the Cobra POC is; worth a skim before 9:30 for shared vocabulary with Chris on data handling.

Latent Space: "AI Is Getting Good at Math. Here's Why." Latent SpaceAnkit Maloo on why models now solve hard problems: compute traded for time, plus a verifier.

Maloo's explanation for the maths results of the last year is mechanical rather than mystical: the systems do depth-first search over candidate proofs with an automated verifier pruning dead branches, so they shine where the solution space is narrow and checkable and struggle where it is open-ended. That framing transfers directly to agent design in general. Give the model a cheap, reliable verifier and a budget to search, and reliability climbs; take the verifier away and you are back to vibes. For pricing analysis, the verifier is the spreadsheet.
