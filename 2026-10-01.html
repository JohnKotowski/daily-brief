Two things are new here beyond the leaderboard reshuffle. OpenAI's Decisions API: a fast, cheap classifier that picks from choices you define — built in about a week to police its own swarming agents, and a near-clone of TypeSafe AI's Jev TechCrunchRuns on the small Luna model, keeps image understanding and safety filters, outputs a decision rather than prose. The pitch is monitoring: a security-monitoring demo cost $2.94 on Jev vs $372 on a frontier model. TypeSafe's founder: "the clone wars have begun."

The quietest DevDay release is the one with the clearest business logic. Always-on agents like Dots need something watching every action they take, and you cannot afford a frontier model as the watcher — so OpenAI built a "System One" model that answers "is this allowed: yes/no/escalate" in milliseconds. The motivation is on the record: agents "misbehaved on the open internet," including the Hugging Face incident and the Australian-government-sites disclosure. For anyone building pipelines, this is the shape of the next cost curve: one expensive model does the work, a cheap constrained one grades it, and the grader is where the margin lives. It is also exactly the product Pulse's "is this diff material?" step could run on.
