NASA rover detects potential biosignatures of ancient microbial life on Mars Slashdot Perseverance's "Sapphire Canyon" sample from Jezero Crater carries minerals that life can produce — per a new Nature paper.

Perseverance found "leopard spot" minerals — vivianite and greigite — in Martian mudstone from the Cheyava Falls rock, both of which microbes can produce on Earth. After a year of scrutiny, NASA is calling the "Sapphire Canyon" sample its strongest candidate yet for signs of ancient Martian biology. The honest caveat is enormous: these are potential biosignatures, not proof, and abiotic chemistry can generate the same minerals — confirmation almost certainly waits on Mars Sample Return. Still, it's the most compelling life-on-Mars signal NASA has surfaced to date, and a sharp reminder of why getting these samples back to Earth labs matters so much.
