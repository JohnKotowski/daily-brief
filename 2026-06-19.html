MIT researchers built "Fractal," an OS just to study how chips really work MIT News · HN 173 A from-scratch kernel that strips away OS background noise to observe processor behavior with unusual clarity.

MIT researchers wrote Fractal, a custom OS kernel built from the ground up purely to observe how modern processors behave. Their reasoning: general-purpose OSes like Linux and macOS inject so much background activity that a chip's actual microarchitectural behavior gets buried in noise. By owning the entire software stack, they can isolate timing, caching, and speculation effects that matter for both performance and security research — think Spectre-class side channels. A niche tool, but the payoff is cleaner science on the hardware everything else runs on, and likely sharper insight into the vulnerabilities hiding in speculative execution.
