"AI Models Can Provably Hide Arbitrary Capabilities" — an encrypted sub-network in the weights that only a secret trigger unlocks, resistant to fine-tuning, noising and adversarial training arXiv via Hacker News · 200 pts Draguns, Motwani, Douglas and Schroeder de Witt argue elicitation-based safety audits have a hard floor: you cannot prompt out what is cryptographically locked.

Current dangerous-capability audits assume that if a model can do something, the right prompt or fine-tune will surface it. This paper builds the counterexample: a "digital locker" that embeds a hidden network in a model's weights, provably hard to find or interpret even with full weight access, and activated only by a secret key. On a small chemistry-prediction task standing in for CBRN capability, the hidden behaviour survived fine-tuning, weight noise, gradient trigger search and latent adversarial training. The practical implication is for supply chain, not prompts — open weights from an untrusted source can carry things no eval will find — which is the argument for provenance over red-teaming. Lands the same week as the White House incident-reporting mandate.

"AI is a tool. Using it well takes skill." — a reply to Rich Hickey's "it's automation, not a tool", and the line "Tools can amplify poor judgment as readily as good judgment" yyhh.org via Hacker News · 397 pts Hard to control doesn't mean not a tool; compilers and spreadsheets automate too. The useful question is whether the operator directs, evaluates and fixes — or hands off and doesn't check.

The essay takes Hickey's argument seriously and then dismantles it: a thing a human can wield to a purpose is a tool, even if it has its own behaviour, and most tools we trust (compilers, spreadsheets, autopilots) are heavily automated. What separates good use from bad is skill — directing the system, judging the output, catching and repairing errors — and that skill is unevenly distributed, which is why the same model produces Torvalds' guitar pedal and the kernel's patch flood. It also calls out anthropomorphic language ("the model conspired to…") as a way of hiding engineering problems. Short, calm, and the best framing of the week's "does it help?" studies.

Latent Space — Anthropic's "dynamic workflows": the lead Claude agent writes the program that spawns, coordinates and collects its own subagents, instead of a developer wiring a fixed roster Latent Space · Oct 12 Harrison Stall and CJ Avilla from Anthropic at AI Engineer NYC: "the next version of multi-agent orchestration".
