Japan demonstrates 90% lithium recovery from used EV batteries HN 433 A potential step-change for battery recycling economics — and for supply-chain independence from mined lithium.

Japanese researchers demonstrated a process recovering up to 90% of the lithium in spent EV batteries, well above the recovery rates that make today's recycling mostly a cobalt-and-nickel business. If it scales, the economics flip: end-of-life packs become a domestic lithium mine, which matters enormously for countries (Japan, the EU) with EV fleets but no lithium deposits. The first EV wave's batteries start aging out in volume in the late 2020s, so timing-wise this lands exactly when feedstock supply gets interesting.
