Apple and OpenEvidence launch a study app that answers health questions from clinical sources TechCrunchApple enters health AI through a research study with grounded sources, not a general chatbot.

Apple is partnering with OpenEvidence on a research app that answers everyday medical questions, grounded in sources such as NEJM, JAMA and Nature. The study targets up to 200,000 US participants over 21 for more than 12 months, on iPhone 15 or later with Apple Intelligence enabled. Participants can share HealthKit data. Apple says the data is anonymized and not used for training, and that OpenEvidence deletes queries and responses after 24 hours.

Essay: all AI-generated code should be open source Jakub Jankiewicz · HN 1,166Who owns agent-written code is unsettled, and the size of the thread shows how many people care.

The author argues that models were trained on open-source code and Creative Commons-licensed Stack Overflow answers without attribution, so turning their output into proprietary code amounts to "license laundering". He adds a legal argument: purely AI-generated work is arguably not copyrightable in the US, citing the monkey-selfie precedent, though countries such as the UK differ. He proposes releasing unreviewed, prompt-only code under an open-source licence or CC0.
