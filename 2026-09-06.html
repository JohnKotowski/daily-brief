"Large Language Models as a Cognitive Virus" — Solé, Krakauer and Levin model LLM adoption as an epidemic with tipping pointsarXiv · HN 269 Users move between uncoupled, coupled and persistently dependent states; past a threshold, small adoption increases flip whole populations into dependence.

Nine authors including Ricard Solé, David Krakauer (Santa Fe Institute) and Michael Levin borrow epidemiology to ask what happens when a population adopts LLMs. The model produces lock-in: beyond a critical adoption level, the transition to persistent dependence is abrupt and comes with "abrupt losses in cognitive competence" at the population level. The constructive half identifies "cognitive immunization" conditions — keep transmission low and keep use reversible — which is a testable design principle rather than a lament. Serious names, and a paper you will hear cited in every "is AI making us dumber" argument this fall.
