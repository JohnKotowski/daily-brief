Fable 5.1 cracks a 370-year-old cipher in 44 minutes — the key was the book itself Vals.ai · HN 901Sir Thomas Urquhart's 1653 Cyphral Distich sat on Klaus Schmeh's top-50 unsolved list; the model solved it unattended in 176k tokens.

Vals gave Claude Fable 5.1 an open task with no hints: recover the plaintext of the 64-number cryptogram at the end of Urquhart's Logopandecteision, posed as an open problem in 1899 and attacked since with frequency analysis and homophonic substitution. All of those assumed an external key. The model noticed what centuries of humans skipped: the cipher follows 32 "Proquiritations", each line has 32 numbers, and the accompanying poem promises the reader "the Author's minde" — so the i-th number indexes a word in the i-th Proquiritation, take its first letter. Out falls O GOD UPHOLD KING CHARLS THE SECOND AND MAKE HIM THE SUPREME RULER OF THIS LAND. The post is two weeks old but hit 900 points on HN this weekend, and the lesson is the one that matters for agents generally: the win came from reading the surrounding context, not from more compute on the puzzle.
