Fireworks co-founder: most companies can skip frontier models once token spend passes $10,000 a month TechCrunchA pricing argument: buyers are starting to compare cost per task, not cost per token.

Interviewed at Slush, Chenyu Zhao said open-weight models are now three to six months behind the frontier. His rule of thumb is that using the best model is the low-risk choice before product-market fit, and that at 10,000 to 100,000 users and $10,000 to $100,000 a month in tokens it is time to look at open models. He expects a "two-model world" with frontier models for planning and cheaper ones for routine work, which is why routing is getting attention. Fireworks sells exactly this, so he is not neutral, and the company disclosed two security incidents this autumn; Notion has disconnected from it.

Google signs with Blue Origin to launch data-centre satellites, first flight in early 2029 Slashdot · EngadgetOrbital compute moves from a research paper to a launch contract.

Under Project Suncatcher, each satellite will carry roughly a data-centre rack's worth of capacity, including Google's own tensor processing units, and will fly on New Glenn rockets into a sun-synchronous orbit for near-constant solar power. Google calls it a "long-term moonshot" to test whether AI infrastructure can run in space, away from the pressure data centres put on grids, land and cooling. Nothing launches for more than two years, so this is an experiment with a date, not a product.
