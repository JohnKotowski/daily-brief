Goodfire: "Neural networks are already clean" — most of the evidence for polysemanticity was misread, and most neurons can be understood directly even at trillion-parameter scale Goodfire · HN 673 pts A 65-minute essay with the first published neuron-only attribution graph of a multi-trillion-parameter model (Kimi K3). swyx's framing: a global lowering of P(doom).

Nick Cammarata and Goodfire argue that the canonical cases of polysemanticity and superposition — the "knots" of neurons that seemed to pack arbitrary unrelated concepts together and resisted interpretation — mostly dissolve under further analysis into a single motif they call recursive generalisation. Their claim is that most neurons in modern models, dense and MoE alike and up to trillions of parameters, can now be understood directly, and they back it with what they say is the first neuron-only attribution graph of a multi-trillion-parameter model, built on Kimi K3. Latent Space's take is that the analysis was agent-driven and that if it holds, interpretability and alignment both get materially easier. The HN thread has the expected pushback that "clean under this method" is not the same as "clean," but it is the most-discussed research post of the week.

Citizen Lab confirms the first zero-click spyware delivered through targeted ads: at least 45 Al Jazeera machines infected with no click required TechCrunch An Israeli ad-tech firm's platform let spyware makers Candiru and Bindecy target specific devices through ordinary ad networks. The governments behind it are unknown.

Citizen Lab's report, which it calls AdFection, is the first public confirmation of a technique that has been rumoured for years: using the ad-delivery stack to put an exploit in front of a specific person's device. Discover-Tech, an Israeli company, ran a platform called Controlium that let spyware vendors Candiru and Bindecy target chosen devices through ad networks; at least 45 Windows machines at Al Jazeera were infected across 2024 and 2025 with no user interaction. Which governments were the customers is not established. The broad-impact point is that every ad-blocker argument just changed from "annoyance and tracking" to "attack surface," and that the ad networks involved were not compromised — they were used as designed.

The leap second is dead: the CGPM votes to stop inserting them into UTC, allowing drift of up to an hour before any correction BIPM · HN 172 pts Resolution 5 of the 28th General Conference on Weights and Measures ends 30 years of argument over leap-second outages. A "leap hour" will not be needed for centuries.

Resolution 5 ends the practice of adding leap seconds to Coordinated Universal Time to keep it within 0.9 seconds of Earth's rotation. UTC may now drift up to one hour from astronomical time before a correction is considered, which at current rates will not happen for hundreds to thousands of years. The practical effect is that the class of outages caused by a 61-second minute — the 2012 Reddit and Linux kernel incidents, the 2017 Cloudflare DNS bug — goes away, and the "smeared" clocks Google and Amazon run can be retired. A note for anyone pulling this from feeds: the Slashdot RSS item for this story is malformed and links to the Slashdot homepage, so the link here is the BIPM resolution itself.
