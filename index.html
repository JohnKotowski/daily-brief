Apple adds Mac permission controls aimed at AI agents in macOS 27.3 TechCrunchA platform-level response to desktop agents, and it changes how Claude or Codex get access on your Mac.

Apple is splitting Device Control and Data Access into two settings, "System & App Protection Override" and Accessibility, with a single way to grant permissions and clearer warnings about Full Disk Access. Users can make an app ask every time it needs access, and an indicator shows when one app is controlling another. The changes ship in macOS 27.3 and do not stop agents from being installed.

OpenCode Browse lets coding agents drive your real, logged-in browser OpenCode · HN 250An open-source take on the agent-in-your-browser pattern that works with any MCP client.

Browse is an MCP server plus a Chromium extension. Agents work in background tab groups in your existing browser, with your tabs, logins and extensions, where they read, click, type, screenshot and record. For logins, two-factor prompts or payments the agent hands the tab back and resumes when you click Continue, and a site allowlist can confine it. It works with OpenCode, Claude Code, Cursor, Codex and Zed across Chrome, Edge, Brave and Arc. This is the product page, so the description is the vendor's own.

US export controls now cover remote access to AI chips, not just shipments TechCrunchThe rule reaches offshore cloud compute that serves Chinese companies.

A Commerce Department interim rule requires a licence to export, re-export or transfer AI chips and servers to data centres anywhere in the world if they may supply compute to companies in China or other embargoed countries. It applies above a threshold of roughly 750 Nvidia H100 or H200 chips and covers access arranged through brokers. It lands days after Jensen Huang's resignation.

Windows 11 reports which coding agents and developer tools you run Xusheng Li · HN 603Microsoft competes in coding agents and collects rival-tool usage by default.

A developer reverse-engineered a Windows component and found it matching process names for command-line agents (copilot, claude, codex) and desktop ones (Claude, Codex, Cursor, Antigravity), plus tools such as python, node and VS Code, including under WSL. Matches are stored locally and sent as part of optional diagnostic data, which was switched on by default in a clean US install of Windows 11 Home. It can be turned off under Settings, Privacy & security, Diagnostics & feedback. This is one researcher's analysis and Microsoft has not commented in the post.

Microsoft traces last week's 365 outage to a defect shipped in July; JPMorgan Chase has its own failure The Register · HN 323A months-old latent bug and thin load testing took down a global service.

Microsoft's preliminary report says a July update to its network gateway servers caused lock contention that got worse on newer machines with more cores, which its load testing had under-represented. A Teams failover and about 14% of gateway capacity being out set off a cascade that left more than 80% of servers unresponsive. Rollback was not possible, so engineers patched the patch. Separately, Chase said a software bug in an on-premise hardware system, not an attack, delayed transactions for thousands of customers on Monday, with other banks affected where Chase was the counterparty.

Study: 403 US hyperscale data centres use more electricity than Michigan WiredThe first facility-level estimate in a sector with no disclosure requirement.

A Harvard-led study in Nature Sustainability modelled 403 hyperscale facilities operating between May 2024 and April 2025, using industry research and satellite data. Most of their electricity came from fossil fuels, and emissions are three times the level before the AI boom. Virginia, Oregon, Ohio and Iowa account for more than half the energy use. Governments do not track data-centre power separately, and the authors say the numbers may already be out of date.

Seven things AI-native companies do differently with agents AI Daily BriefA working checklist for a small team: invest in shared context and skills, not a roster of named agents.

NLW pulls together what Stripe, Shopify, Uber, Ramp and Cloudflare have published about running agents in production. The shared practices include giving agents the whole picture, letting signals instead of prompts start work, orchestrators that start sub-agents, agents checking each other, and human review scaled to risk. His view is that "agents as teammates" is the wrong model and that the lasting asset is context. He cites Uber's library of 3,600 skills running 30,000 times a day.
