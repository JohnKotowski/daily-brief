Two Amazon engineers replaced Claude Sonnet 4.6 with "Strands Decider" for 20-category ticket labelling in one of Amazon's largest internal ticketing systems: about $500 instead of $22,200 per million tickets, 897 ms instead of 4,215 ms, same or better accuracy Strands AgentsA decision model returns a probability per option plus a confidence score instead of generating text.

Strands Decider is not a chat model; it is a classifier that takes the options and returns a distribution over them, which is why it uses 70% fewer tokens and why latency drops below a second. The held-out comparison matched or beat Sonnet on the 20 categories. Worth a look for the PricingSaaS pipeline wherever a model is being asked "which of these N" rather than "write this": Pulse diff classification, noise filtering and routing are all that shape, and the EOS One and Whalesync corrections in your inbox are the cost of getting such a step wrong.
