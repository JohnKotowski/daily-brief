1Password: an attacker copied customer names and emails; vaults and keys were not touched 1Password · HN 1431Entry was through an employee's corporate GitHub account. No action is required, but expect phishing.

1Password says it began investigating on Nov 19 after signs that an attacker had taken over an employee's corporate GitHub account. The attacker read source code, reached the CI/CD system and pushed malicious code to backend services, which downloaded account records for a subset of customers: names, sign-in email addresses, account identifiers, creation and last sign-in times, and tokens from some emailed invitation and recovery links. Vault contents and the keys protecting them were not accessed, and 1Password does not hold account passwords or secret keys. The company has rotated internal credentials and lengthened the emailed verification code from six digits to ten alphanumeric characters. Its advice is that you do not need to change anything, and that you should distrust unexpected emails about 1Password, such as invitations or recovery requests you did not start. 1Password is on my list of your regular senders, so I assume you have an account and the advice applies to you; I found no mail from them in the work inboxes this week.
