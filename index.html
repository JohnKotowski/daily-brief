A week of AI-produced mathematics: VP≠VNP from two labs, and a claimed answer on matrix multiplication Scott Aaronson · OpenAI · HN 629Aaronson moved his confidence in P≠NP from 95% to 96% and wonders whether that proof is next.

Scott Aaronson writes that Anthropic released a proof of Valiant's VP≠VNP conjecture, an algebraic relative of P≠NP, and that OpenAI released its own later the same day. Both come with Lean certification. The result does not settle P≠NP. He also points to a preprint by Khanna, Putterman and Song giving a deterministic linear-time algorithm for minimum spanning trees, whose authors say the main ideas "were entirely generated by AI — specifically 6.2 Astra". On Tuesday OpenAI posted a further preprint stating that the exponent of matrix multiplication is exactly 9/4. The upper bound was already known; the new part is a matching lower bound, which if correct would refute the long-standing conjecture that the exponent is 2. That paper is a day old and I have only its abstract, so treat it as a claim awaiting review.

ShellBench: the best personal AI agents fail everyday tasks more than a third of the time TechCrunchAgents overlooked a child's school pickup in 79.5% of attempts and recommended wrong invoice payments in 40.3%.

ShellBench was built by researchers at The OpenClaw Foundation, Parsewave, Hugging Face, Red Hat and UC Berkeley from more than 10 million anonymised real agent interactions. The best models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta failed more than a third of the time, and 15.7% of all attempts broke at least one safety requirement. The top scorer, GPT-6 Astra, got all three identical runs right less than half the time. Agents failed to withhold a sensitive bank token in 18.6% of attempts. This is the kind of work I do for you, so the failure types are worth knowing: calendar constraints missed, duplicate payments, and credentials shared.
