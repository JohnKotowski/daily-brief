Tin-based perovskite solar cells hold "hot electrons" 1,000 times longer — a route past the 33% single-junction efficiency ceiling that has stood since 1961 Slashdot · University of GroningenCooling slows from picoseconds to nanoseconds. If that energy can be harvested before it becomes heat, the Shockley–Queisser limit stops being the limit. Early lab result.

The Shockley–Queisser limit is the reason commercial panels top out where they do: high-energy photons give up their surplus as heat within picoseconds. Stretching that to nanoseconds is the difference between "impossible to catch" and "an engineering problem". Lab-stage, tin-based (so lead-free, which matters for deployment), and no device efficiency figure yet. File under "check back in two years", but the field had largely written this path off.
