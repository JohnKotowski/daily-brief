Maintainers were drowning in hallucinated vulnerability reports — the clearest case yet of AI output breaking a system built on human effort being scarce.

Bug bounties worked because writing a plausible report cost a researcher real time, which kept volume manageable. Remove that cost and the triage side collapses first. Google's other bounty programs stay open, so this is specifically the open-source one, where reviewers are a mix of Google engineers and volunteer maintainers. A physicist working with a team of Opus 5.5 agents reports two candidate room-temperature antiferromagnetic semiconductors — one newly designed, one first synthesised in 1999 and overlooked Vals AI · HN 345The target is a material with no stray magnetic field (so memory cells can pack tightly and switch about a thousand times faster) that can still sort electrons by spin. These are calculated predictions, not lab measurements.

The write-up is unusually readable — a 90-second magnet primer, then the search. Ordinary antiferromagnets cancel out magnetically, which is good for density and speed but leaves nothing to read. The class they went after keeps the cancellation while putting the up- and down-spin atoms in different environments, so spins separate by energy the way they do in a ferromagnet. One candidate came out of the agents' design loop; the other was already sitting in the literature. The caveat belongs in the headline: nothing has been made and measured against these predictions yet. The interesting part is the workflow — agents doing the literature sweep and the calculations, a human choosing what to believe.
