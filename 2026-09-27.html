After 40 years, Excel cells can hold lists and arrays — brace syntax {1;2;3}, Ctrl+J to insert a list, new HAS / HASANY / HASALL / FLATTEN functions SlashdotVia Microsoft's 365 Insider blog. Slashdot's commenters split between "finally" and "spreadsheets in your spreadsheet". Not AI — but it's the biggest change to the cell model since the product shipped.

One value per cell has been Excel's founding constraint, and every workaround (comma-joined strings, helper columns, TEXTSPLIT gymnastics) exists because of it. Multi-value cells with membership functions remove a whole class of that. The sceptics have a point about printing and about people building databases in spreadsheets, but that ship sailed in 1995. Rolling out through the Insider channel first; no GA date in the post.

Why it matters to you: the PuckFinder programme sheets and the Wildcats roster tabs are full of exactly the comma-joined-list pattern this replaces.

Innovation & Infra

AI is finding so many Linux kernel bugs that Canonical has moved Ubuntu to a two-week stable security cycle — target: a safe workaround within 24–48 hours of disclosure SlashdotCanonical: bug discovery went "from a manual, time-intensive process into a highly automated engine". The kernel becoming its own CVE authority multiplied the volume further.

Canonical is careful to say this is better detection, not worse code — "we are getting much better at finding and cataloging problems that may have previously gone unnoticed." But the operational effect is the same: the interval between disclosure and exploit is shrinking, so the distro has to ship mitigations before patches and on a fixed cadence. For anyone running servers, biweekly kernel SRUs are now the baseline; for the rest of the industry it's a preview of what AI-scale vulnerability discovery does to every maintenance schedule.
