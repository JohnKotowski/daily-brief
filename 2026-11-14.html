AI helps decode Gödel's shorthand notebooks, said to hold hundreds of unknown theorems The Guardian1,009 pages in obsolete shorthand; a human project had been managing about a page a week.

Since 2017 logician Jan von Plato's Helsinki University project has worked through 16 Gödel notebooks from the early 1940s, much of it in Gabelsberger shorthand. In September he enlisted Claude, which produced a digest of the whole material within a couple of days. Von Plato says it shows hundreds of previously unknown proved theorems, dozens of anticipations of results not known until the 1970s and 80s, and 14 themes in logic that have not been pursued. Those are his claims.
