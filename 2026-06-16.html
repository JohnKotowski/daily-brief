Commonwealth Fusion publishes the full physics case for its 400 MW ARC plant Slashdot Five peer-reviewed papers detail molten-salt blankets, HTS magnets and 15-minute pulses for a net-400MW reactor.

Commonwealth Fusion Systems published five peer-reviewed papers in the Journal of Plasma Physics laying out the engineering case for ARC, its planned 400 MW (net-to-grid) power plant that follows the SPARC tokamak now under construction. The design leans on commercially available high-temperature superconducting magnets, a molten-salt blanket that both extracts neutron heat and breeds replacement tritium, and a 15-minute-pulse / 1-minute-reset cycle, with a vessel engineered to split in half for the 1–2 year wall swaps fusion has always struggled with. What matters is the shift from theory to buildable engineering using off-the-shelf superconductors — addressing material degradation and continuous operation, fusion's perennial blockers. With AI-driven demand straining grids and data-center opposition mounting, credible firm clean baseload is suddenly strategic. Watch SPARC's net-energy milestone as the next proof point.
