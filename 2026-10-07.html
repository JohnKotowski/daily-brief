Attackers compromised the registries for .gh, .sl and .as, rewrote DNS, and got real TLS certificates for Google and other large services issued — Let's Encrypt among the CAs Ars Technica · HN Not a CA breach: they deleted the domains' CAA records during the hijack, so any CA would issue. Google revoked and locked issuance to its own CA.

The weak link was the country-code top-level domains, not the certificate authorities: with registry access, the attackers changed DNS records for Google-owned names under .gh, .sl and .as, stripped the CAA records that limit which CAs may issue, and then requested certificates through normal validation. Google revoked the counterfeit certs and added CAA records restricting issuance to Google Trust Services; every other domain under those three ccTLDs was equally exposed for the duration. The practical takeaway for anyone running a domain: set CAA records, and monitor certificate transparency logs for your names.
