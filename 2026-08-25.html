MS Paint and Photos stamp a server-issued GUID into locally generated images Xusheng Li The "visible watermark" toggle doesn't control it, and the prompt goes to a Microsoft server even when generation is local.

A careful piece of reverse engineering: Paint and Photos ship local AI models, but they still send your prompt to a remote server for moderation. The server returns a moderated prompt plus a GUID, and that GUID gets embedded into the pixels of the locally generated image as an invisible watermark. The separate visible-watermark setting doesn't govern it. Even on Copilot+ PCs — where generation genuinely runs on-device — moderation stays remote. Microsoft does disclose C2PA metadata; the pixel-level per-generation identifier is the part nobody signed up for. Good reminder that "runs locally" and "phones home" are orthogonal claims.

The evolution of the agent harness — and why it keeps getting eaten by the model Latent Space Dan McAteer traces harness design from Devin through Claude Code, and argues the endgame is a harness for human attention, not for the model.

The framing is a race between two curves: model capability and harness scaffolding. Early agents like the first Devin handed autonomy straight to the model and hit roughly a 15% success rate — so IDEs correctly retreated, pulling the harness below the model curve and keeping the human in the loop. Then o1 landed at the end of 2024 and the gap inverted, creating a capability overhang; Claude Code was the first harness built to seize that, dropping the brakes. McAteer's forward call is that models keep absorbing harness logic into their weights, so the thing left to design is the interface that manages your attention across many running agents. If you're building agent tooling, this is the map.

A malicious LLM could take over the machine its own weights run on Boyd Kane Not prompt injection into the agent — an attack from the model's output tokens down into the inference engine itself.

The usual threat model puts the agent harness (Claude Code, Codex) on one machine and the weights on a GPU box somewhere else, and worries about the harness. Kane inverts it: the GPU host is the far more valuable target — it has frontier-scale compute, direct access to the weights, and privileged network position inside the datacentre. The attack he explores is a model emitting a token sequence crafted to exploit the inference engine (vLLM and friends) that is parsing its own output. It's a genuinely under-examined seam, and it lands the same week Ben Thompson argues offence structurally outruns defence in agentic security. Read them together.

After ~50 years, physicists may have finally cornered a particle made of pure force Slashdot / ScienceAlert Evidence that X(2370) is dominated by a glueball state — matter built entirely from force carriers, no quarks.

Quantum chromodynamics has predicted "glueballs" — particles composed purely of gluons, the force carriers that bind quarks — for close to five decades, and nobody could produce clean evidence of one. A collaboration at a Beijing collider now reports the strongest case yet that the known particle X(2370) is dominated by exactly such a state, in a preprint presented at the International Conference on High Energy Physics in Brazil. It is the only category of particle in nature made entirely of force rather than of stuff, which makes it one of the more genuinely strange results of the year.
