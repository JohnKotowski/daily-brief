Four-hour batteries are now cheaper than the open-cycle gas turbines data centers have been ordering, in all 43 markets Wood Mackenzie surveyed — and turbine lead times run two to four years TechCrunch Solar is the cheapest new power everywhere in the study; AI demand is what pushed gas over the line.

Wood Mackenzie's new cost study finds that four-hour battery storage undercuts open-cycle gas turbines in every one of the 43 markets it looked at, with solar the cheapest new generation in all of them. The reason is partly that batteries kept getting cheaper and partly that AI data-center demand has inflated gas-turbine prices and stretched delivery to two to four years, and into the early 2030s for combined-cycle units. For hyperscalers that means the "gas bridge" is now the slow, expensive option and storage-first is the fast one. Worth reading next to the Ontario grid questions that come up every time a new data center is announced near Ottawa.
