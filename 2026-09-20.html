Grant Sanderson (3Blue1Brown), guest-posting on Terence Tao's blog: if AI can produce proofs, mathematics should start formally crediting explanation Terence Tao's blog · HN 351 ptsProofs were a proxy for understanding; now that the proxy is cheap, define and reward "motivated explanations" on par with new results.

Sanderson's argument, published Friday on Tao's blog (Tao notes it was converted between formats using AI), is that with models generating proofs without understanding, proof loses its value as the field's proxy for its real goal — furthering human understanding — so mathematics should define and credit "motivated explanations" (answers to "how would you think of that?") as first-class contributions. He leans on Gowers's Princeton Companion, Thurston's On Proof and Progress, Timothy Chow's forcing paper and the recent Alexeev–Tao work on Erdős Problem 1196. Concrete proposals: let PhD students present solutions as explanatory talks, publish a "modern Hilbert problems" list of unsolved exposition problems, write standards for motivated explanation, found journals for it, and weight tenure toward great exposition. It reads as the constructive sequel to Thursday's Gowers piece on the Fields-medallists' letter.

Why it matters to you: the same reframing applies to software and to hiring — when the model produces the artefact, the scarce human value shifts to explanation, judgment and taste, which is what a product roadmap and a job description should reward.
