Wiles's 1995 proof ran 129 pages and took months to verify by hand. Tianyi Peng's group at Columbia set Claude loose on the Lean formalization and it went end to end in eleven days. The interesting part is not the theorem — it is that machine-checked proof turns "trust the referee" into "run the checker," and this is the first time an AI has carried a landmark result all the way through. Anthropic's post is careful about what this does and doesn't mean for research mathematics; the HN thread has working mathematicians arguing both sides. The Lean repo is on GitHub alongside.

A useful corrective to launch-day demos. When an agent drives a graphical CAD tool, most of its context is coordinates and menu state, so you end up benchmarking computer use rather than electronics. EEBench has the model edit the circuit as code, build it, simulate, inspect the failure and iterate — and finds the models are much better than the KiCad footage suggests.
