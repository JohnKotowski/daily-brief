Ollaya: an open-source local runtime for "decision models" — Jev-style single-pass classifiers answering typed questions in 8–10 ms on your own GPU Hacker News · 442 ptsDrop-in compatible with the TypeSafe SDK, so existing Jev code runs unchanged. ~250 ms hosted vs ~10 ms local on an RTX 4090.

Decision models are the cheapest layer of agent plumbing — routing, guardrails, scoring, "is this JSON what I think it is" — answered in a single forward pass rather than a generation loop. TypeSafe's hosted Jev popularised the pattern; Ollaya is the Ollama-shaped answer: pull an open model, run it locally, keep the same API. The HN thread is mostly people describing where they'd already put one (pre-filtering tool calls, checking form submissions before an LLM ever sees them) and asking how the licensing shakes out. Quiet product day otherwise — the Opus 5.5 / GPT-6 wave from earlier this week is still what everyone is benchmarking.

Why it matters to you: the inbox-sweep noise filter is a hand-maintained list of senders. A local decision model that answers "is this human mail?" in 10 ms is exactly the kind of thing that would replace it — worth a Saturday-afternoon poke if you're bored.

A new classical attack forges RSA signatures without factoring the key — deprecated 1024-bit keys fell in months on an academic cluster SlashdotNadia Heninger (UCSD) among the authors. Current implementations are safe for now; cryptographers call it a conceptual break that strengthens the case for leaving RSA during the post-quantum transition.

Not a quantum result. The attack targets the signature construction rather than the modulus, cutting the compute needed by orders of magnitude compared with factoring. Nothing in production 2048-bit-and-up RSA is at risk today, but the point is trajectory: the "RSA is fine until quantum" assumption has just lost its safety margin, and migration timelines that were pinned to quantum readiness now have a second reason to move.
